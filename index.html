Why it is hard

“Was it blocked?” is three questions,
and most systems answer one.

TrailMQ separates them on purpose, and says so in its own Activity view: Outcome says whether the operation was permitted. Evidence says whether it was written down. Integrity says whether the validated chain covers that record. They are three different questions.

Outcome

Was it permitted?

Two independent gates decide it: the role's permission, and the topic rule that brings the topic into scope. Either one can refuse.

Evidence

Was it written down?

Refusals, sign-ins and administrative changes are always recorded. Recording the messages themselves stays a per-topic decision.

Integrity

Is the record covered?

A hash-linked chain covers system and action entries and reports its own scope. MQTT decision records sit outside it, and are labelled that way.

Conflating these three is how a system ends up claiming more than it can show.

Then go and trigger your own denial

Open http://localhost/trailmq/, sign in as testadmin, and publish to a topic your role does not cover:

mosquitto_pub -h localhost -p 8883 --cafile "$CA" \
  -u testuser -P "$PW" \
  -t 'restricted/ops/config' -q 1 -m '{"setpoint":42}'

Then open Activity and filter Outcome → Denied. Your attempt is the top row, with your client id and the reason attached.

The run takes about 30 seconds once the images are pulled. No local MQTT client is required — verify falls back to a temporary Docker client.

Limits

What TrailMQ does not claim

These are the boundaries the product states in its own interface, next to the verdict. They are documentation, not fine print.

Not proof of delivery

A recorded allow means TrailMQ authorized and accepted the operation at the broker. It does not show payloads or name which subscribers received them, and Preview counters are not delivery proof. ./trailmq verify checks delivery with a real subscriber instead.

Not tamper-evident for MQTT decisions

The hash-linked chain covers system and action entries — sign-ins, administrative changes, identity, role, policy and topic-rule changes. It is not externally anchored, not digitally signed, and does not walk MQTT message evidence, including publish and subscribe refusals. It demonstrates internal consistency, not third-party custody.

Not a compliance certification

TrailMQ is a technical building block. It is not WORM storage, not a notarization service, not a CE declaration, not a GMP/GxP validation or Annex 11 package, and not a 21 CFR Part 11 package. Validation and procedural controls remain yours.

Technical detail

How access is decided

Every MQTT action must pass two independent gates. A role with publish:* can still be refused by the second one.

Gate 1

Role permission

The identity's role allows the action and the MQTT topic filter.

AND
Gate 2

Topic rule

A rule brings the requested path into scope for a role the identity holds.
