    1. 1 of 6

      A sensor publishes to a topic its role does not cover

      terminal · sensor
      # authenticated as testuser, whose role is "publisher"
mosquitto_pub -h localhost -p 8883 --cafile "$CA" \
  -u testuser -P "$USER_PW" \
  -t 'restricted/ops/config' -q 1 -m '{"setpoint":42}'

      The credentials are valid. Authentication is not the problem here — which is exactly what makes this case annoying to debug.

    2. 2 of 6

      The client gets a protocol error, not an authorization error

      terminal · sensor
      Error: A network protocol error occurred when communicating with the broker.

      At QoS 1 the broker refuses to acknowledge the publish and drops the connection, so most clients surface it as a transport fault. At QoS 0 the same command exits cleanly and the message is still discarded — there is no acknowledgement to refuse with. This is the gap the recorded decision closes.

    3. 3 of 6

      The broker attributed the refusal as it made it

      ./trailmq logs backend | grep ACLMon
      [ACLMon] DENY user="testuser" roles=[publisher] action=publish topic="restricted/ops/config"

      Identity, role, action and topic are on the decision itself — not reassembled afterwards from a connection log and an ACL file.

    5. 5 of 6

      Permitted, written down, and covered are three different answers

      Outcome Denied Was the operation permitted?
      Evidence Recorded Was it written down?
      Integrity Outside validated scope Does the validated chain cover it?

      TrailMQ labels this record Outside validated scope rather than quietly implying the chain covers it. Next to this, the Access screen resolves the effective rule for the topic, so the fix is a rule you write deliberately — not a guess.

    6. 6 of 6

      And the record tells you what it is worth

      The refusal you just read sits outside the chain that TrailMQ validates. The chain covers system and action entries — sign-ins, administrative changes, policy and topic-rule changes — and it is neither externally anchored nor digitally signed, so it shows internal consistency, not third-party custody.
